United Airlines says it is banning personal AI agents, the apps that book and buy things for you, from making flight reservations.

## Why United says it's a problem

To book a flight, an agent needs your login and your payment details. United says sharing those credentials can expose sensitive information, and that a bot's actions could lead to unintended purchases, account changes, or use of your MileagePlus miles.

## The money reason

One airline analyst, Henry Harteveldt of Atmosphere Research Group, points out that flight searches cost airlines money, and AI bots search a lot. In his view United wants to avoid the expense of a surge of bot searches. That is the analyst's view, not United's statement.

## What still works

United fares stay bookable through flight aggregators such as Kayak, Hopper and Google Flights. Let your AI find the deal, then click buy yourself.
